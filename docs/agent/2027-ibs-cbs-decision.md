# 2027 IBS/CBS Decision for J&L

Last researched: 2026-09-02.

This document records the analysis and current decision about whether J&L should remain in ordinary Simples Nacional treatment for IBS/CBS ("Simples puro") or elect the regular IBS/CBS regime while otherwise remaining in Simples ("Simples híbrido").

This is a planning decision based on the current business model. Re-check the law, official rates, actual expenses, customer tax treatment, and company economics before acting in a later option window.

## Current decision

**Do not elect the regular IBS/CBS regime in September 2026 for January-June 2027.**

For an existing Simples company, no affirmative action is needed merely to keep IBS/CBS inside Simples. The September election is only needed if the company wants IBS/CBS calculated under the regular regime outside the DAS.

Re-evaluate in **March 2027** before the option window for July-December 2027. By then there should be better information about the final 2027 CBS rate, actual 2027 expense credits, the customer's tax treatment, and whether the customer would pay highlighted IBS/CBS in addition to the existing service fee.

## Business-model assumptions behind this decision

The current model assumes no material operational change:

- recurring dentistry service revenue remains around R$ 8.250,00 per month;
- the customer continues paying the same total monthly amount rather than adding regular-regime IBS/CBS on top;
- monthly pró-labore remains around the current R$ 5.000,00 candidate when annual personal-income and company-cash checks continue to support it;
- there are no employees, inventory-heavy operations, or large recurring taxable supplier purchases;
- Factor R remains at or above 0,28, so dentistry is expected to be in Annex III once the payroll history has matured as projected.

If any of these assumptions change, do not reuse the conclusion mechanically.

## What "hybrid" means

A Simples company may elect to remain in Simples while calculating and paying IBS and CBS under the regular regime. In that case:

- IRPJ, CSLL, CPP, ISS and the remaining Simples components continue through the DAS;
- the IBS/CBS portions leave the DAS;
- IBS/CBS are then calculated under the regular regime;
- the company becomes eligible for regular-regime input credits subject to the statutory rules and documentation requirements.

Official guidance says an existing Simples company only needs to file the September option if it wants IBS/CBS outside Simples. For the first semester of 2027 the election window is 1-30 September 2026; an election may be cancelled through 30 November 2026, subject to later regulatory changes. A company that does not elect for the first semester may reconsider for the second semester in the March 2027 window.

## Pure-Simples baseline under the current projection

For 2027-2028, Annex III keeps a 6,00% first bracket through R$ 180.000,00 of RBT12. Under the statutory 2027-2028 Annex III partition, first-bracket DAS is divided as follows:

- IRPJ 4,00% of DAS;
- CSLL 3,50%;
- CBS 15,43%;
- CPP 43,40%;
- ISS 33,50%;
- IBS 0,17%.

Therefore, when the effective Annex III rate is 6,00%, the CBS + IBS embedded in the DAS equal:

`6,00% × (15,43% + 0,17%) = 0,936% of revenue`

At R$ 8.250,00 of monthly revenue:

- total DAS at 6,00% = R$ 495,00;
- CBS + IBS embedded in that DAS ≈ R$ 77,22;
- remaining Simples components ≈ R$ 417,78.

This is the relevant pure-Simples baseline to compare against hybrid while the company remains in Annex III first bracket.

## Dentistry receives a 60% IBS/CBS rate reduction

Health services receive a 60% reduction in the regular IBS/CBS rates, meaning the applicable regular-regime rate is 40% of the standard rate. The statutory health-service list includes `Serviços odontológicos`, NBS `1.2301.23.00`, which is the same NBS family used by J&L's National NFS-e.

## Break-even before input credits

Because pure Simples embeds approximately 0,936% of revenue as CBS+IBS under the projected Annex III first bracket, the hybrid regime would beat pure Simples on gross IBS/CBS before credits only if:

`40% × regular combined IBS/CBS rate < 0,936%`

So the standard combined regular IBS/CBS rate would have to be below approximately:

`0,936% / 40% = 2,34%`

For 2027-2028, official transition material establishes IBS at 0,1% total, while the 2027 CBS reference rate is determined separately and was not yet final when this analysis was done. Therefore an exact hybrid bill should **not** be hardcoded until the 2027 CBS rate is officially published.

The Ministry/Receita guidance itself notes that the timing of publication of the CBS reference rate may affect the cancellation deadline. Treat that as a signal not to invent a final 2027 CBS percentage prematurely.

## Input credits are the main possible argument for hybrid

Under the regular regime, a taxpayer may appropriate IBS/CBS credits on eligible acquisitions when the statutory conditions are met and the transaction is supported by a valid electronic fiscal document.

If the company pays IBS/CBS through pure Simples instead:

- the Simples company itself cannot appropriate regular IBS/CBS input credits; but
- a customer that is in the regular IBS/CBS regime may take a credit corresponding to the IBS/CBS amount effectively paid through Simples on its acquisition from the Simples supplier, subject to the statutory rules.

Source:

- LC 214/2025, art. 47, especially §§ 3º and 9º:
  https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/lcp/lcp214compilado.htm

### Why J&L currently has weak credit economics

J&L's dominant cost is the working partner's remuneration rather than taxable inventory or a large supply chain. LC 214 explicitly says IBS/CBS do not apply to services by individuals arising from an employment relationship or their work as company administrators. That means the working partner's pró-labore is not itself a purchase carrying IBS/CBS that generates an input credit for J&L.

Source:

- LC 214/2025, art. 6, I:
  https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/lcp/lcp214compilado.htm

Therefore hybrid becomes more interesting only if J&L develops a substantial volume of eligible taxed purchases, for example equipment, supplies, subcontracted services or other business inputs whose IBS/CBS can actually be credited.

Future agents must model credits from **real invoices and actual tax treatment**, not from generic expense totals.

## Customer-credit argument

A B2B customer in the regular regime may prefer a hybrid supplier because a regular-regime invoice can generate a larger explicit IBS/CBS credit than the limited credit associated with IBS/CBS paid through pure Simples.

However, this is not by itself a reason for J&L to volunteer for a higher tax burden. Before considering hybrid for customer-credit reasons, obtain an explicit answer from the customer's accounting department on all of the following:

1. Is the customer actually entitled to and able to use the resulting credit?
2. Does the customer care enough about the larger credit to change commercial terms?
3. Would the customer pay the current service fee **plus** highlighted IBS/CBS, or would it continue paying the same total amount?

If the total monthly payment remains fixed, additional regular-regime IBS/CBS generally comes out of J&L's existing economics rather than being funded by the customer.

## Pricing is a major decision variable

Do not compare pure and hybrid without specifying whether the quoted service price is tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive.

Current planning assumption:

- the recurring customer continues paying the same total monthly amount.

Under that assumption, hybrid is less attractive because J&L absorbs the additional regular-regime IBS/CBS within the same commercial payment.

A fact that could materially change the recommendation is a new commercial agreement where the current service fee remains the base price and regular IBS/CBS is paid **on top** of that amount. If that occurs, rebuild the model from scratch.

LC 214 also excludes IBS/CBS themselves from the IBS/CBS tax base.

Source:

- LC 214/2025, art. 12, § 2º, I, compiled text:
  https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/lcp/lcp214compilado.htm

## Administrative burden

Pure Simples keeps the consumption-tax calculation inside the Simples workflow. Hybrid introduces separate regular IBS/CBS calculations, credits and documentation while the company simultaneously remains in Simples for the other components.

For a very small professional-services company, administrative simplicity has real value and should be included in the decision unless hybrid produces a clear economic benefit.

## Separate 2027 change: cash basis ends

This decision is independent of the change in monthly Simples revenue recognition. Receita announced that the option to determine the monthly Simples base by cash receipts ends from 1 January 2027. Revenue from service operations will generally be considered earned at faturamento/document issuance under the new rule.

This means future agents must stop assuming that the current 2026 cash-basis PGDAS workflow continues into 2027.

## Decision matrix

Under the current conditions:

| Question | Simples puro | Hybrid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expected gross tax burden before credits | Lower baseline under current Annex III projection | Likely higher unless regular rate/credits produce a clear offset |
| Input credits | J&L cannot take regular IBS/CBS credits | Can take eligible documented credits |
| Pró-labore generates input credit | N/A | No |
| Customer regular-regime credit | Limited to IBS/CBS effectively paid through Simples, when legally available | Potentially larger regular-regime credit |
| Administration | Simpler | More complex |
| Same total customer payment | Favors pure Simples | J&L absorbs extra regular IBS/CBS economics |
| Customer agrees to fee + IBS/CBS | No special upside | Could materially improve hybrid economics |

## Stop conditions

Do not act on the September 2026 hybrid option merely because a portal presents it. The recorded current decision is **no election**.

Stop and re-open the analysis immediately if:

- recurring revenue changes materially;
- Factor R falls below 0,28;
- monthly pró-labore/payroll changes materially;
- the company begins making substantial creditable purchases;
- the customer agrees to pay regular IBS/CBS on top of the current fee;
- the customer requests hybrid treatment as part of a renegotiated contract;
- the final CBS rate makes the gross hybrid comparison unexpectedly favorable;
- official option deadlines or transition rules change.
