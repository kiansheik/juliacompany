# Expense Reimbursements

Last verified: 2026-09-29.

This note records the operating rule for expenses paid personally by a worker/partner and later considered for company reimbursement.

## Genuine business expenses

A reimbursement can retain indemnity/non-remuneratory character when it is genuinely a work-related expense incurred on behalf of the company and is properly documented.

The eSocial nature table includes, among others:

- `1621 - Ressarcimento de despesas de viagem, exceto despesas com veículos`: expenses paid by the worker in work travel;
- `1629 - Ressarcimento de outras despesas`: other expenses paid by the worker and later reimbursed.

Source:
https://www.gov.br/esocial/pt-br/documentacao-tecnica/leiautes-esocial-versao-s-1-3-nt-07-2026/tabelas.html

Receita also states that payments described as indemnities/diárias only keep that character when they actually reimburse work-related expenses; if that purpose is lost, they become remuneratory and can be subject to social-security taxation.

Source:
https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/perguntas-frequentes/sped/efd-reinf/efdr/2-eventos-da-efd-reinf/2-13-12-como-podem-ser

For reimbursement, retain private evidence such as:

- provider receipt/invoice/app receipt;
- date;
- origin/destination or business purpose;
- person who paid;
- amount;
- approval/expense report;
- company reimbursement proof.

Do not use a reimbursement label merely to move personal costs through the company.

## Ordinary commute is different

Residence-to-regular-workplace and return is ordinary commuting, not work travel.

Brazil's Vale-Transporte law specifically treats residence-work commuting as a separate employee benefit using public collective transport. The statutory non-taxable treatment applies under that regime; it is not a general rule that any commuting expense or Uber paid for a partner becomes a tax-free company reimbursement.

Source:
https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l7418compilado.htm

For the current working-partner setup, ordinary Uber rides from home to the usual workplace and back should **not** be treated as tax-free business-expense reimbursement merely because the personal car was unavailable.

If the company nevertheless wants to bear those personal commuting costs, get accounting/payroll treatment before payment rather than booking them as an expense reimbursement.

## Business-transport examples that may qualify

Potentially reimbursable when documented and genuinely required by the business:

- workplace to another work site;
- workplace to supplier, bank, government office or client meeting;
- temporary work travel outside the ordinary commute;
- Uber/taxi during a business trip;
- other company errands paid personally.

The distinguishing question is not whether transportation occurred, but whether the company itself had the business expense versus the partner simply getting to/from the usual workplace.

## Current J&L decision

Do not reimburse ordinary home-to-work Uber commuting tax-free.

If a ride list contains mixed purposes, separate ordinary commute from true business rides and reimburse only the business portion supported by receipts/purpose.
